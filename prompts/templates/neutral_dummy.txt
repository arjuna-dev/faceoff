Create a structural diagram, not a mannequin and not a character. Use only flat
basic 2D geometric shapes with solid neutral colors and simple outlines. Do not
draw a face, skin, muscles, breasts, genitals, hands with fingers, clothing folds,
hair, identity, sex, gender, expression, pose personality, shading or anatomy
details. The diagram must not resemble a finished person.

The shapes are placeholders whose only purpose is to show the twelve cells,
joint centers, segment lengths, ownership and relative scale. Future generation
models must replace every shape with a requested character and must never treat
the placeholder color or silhouette as skin, clothing or identity.

Use one flat light gray for head, torso and arm placeholders. Use a second flat
gray for the lower-torso garment-ownership block, thighs and shins. This block
establishes the mandatory lower-body garment: its waistband and upper section
belong to the torso/pelvis cell, then the same garment continues through the thigh
and shin cells. Use a third flat gray for below-ankle footwear shapes.

Keep the exact canvas, magenta cell backgrounds, white frame and gutters, twelve
detached pieces and all 22 cyan markers. No gradients, texture, lighting, labels,
logos or character-specific details.
